## Cannizzaro Reaction

**Full Name:** Cannizzaro Disproportionation
**Reaction equation:** 2 HCHO + NaOH -> HCOONa + CH3OH

### Key Characteristics
- Occurs in aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens (e.g., formaldehyde, benzaldehyde).
- Requires concentrated strong base (NaOH or KOH).
- It is a redox disproportionation: one molecule is oxidized to a carboxylic acid salt, the other is reduced to an alcohol.
- Mechanism involves hydride ion (H-) transfer, which is the rate-determining step.

### Prototype: Formaldehyde Cannizzaro
Reaction name: Cannizzaro reaction of Formaldehyde
Equation: 2 HCHO + NaOH -> HCOONa + CH3OH
Reactants: Formaldehyde (HCHO) molecule 1, Formaldehyde (HCHO) molecule 2, Hydroxide (OH-)
Products: Sodium formate (HCOONa), Methanol (CH3OH)
Mechanism steps:
1. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=O_base, electrophile_atom=C_carbonyl1. The hydroxide ion attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon of the first formaldehyde molecule.
2. BOND_FORM: atom1=O_base, atom2=C_carbonyl1, order=1. A bond forms between the base oxygen and the carbonyl carbon.
3. ELECTRON_PAIR_MOVE: from_atom=C_carbonyl1, to_atom=O_carbonyl1. The pi electrons move to the carbonyl oxygen, forming a tetrahedral intermediate (gem-diol anion).
4. ELECTRON_PAIR_MOVE: from_atom=O_carbonyl1, to_atom=C_carbonyl1. The electrons on the oxygen collapse back down to reform the C=O pi bond.
5. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C_carbonyl1-H_hydride. The C-H bond breaks, releasing a hydride ion (H-).
6. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=H_hydride, electrophile_atom=C_carbonyl2. The hydride ion directly attacks the carbonyl carbon of the second formaldehyde molecule.
7. BOND_FORM: atom1=H_hydride, atom2=C_carbonyl2, order=1. The hydride attaches to the second molecule.
8. ELECTRON_PAIR_MOVE: from_atom=C_carbonyl2, to_atom=O_carbonyl2. The pi electrons on the second molecule move to the oxygen, forming a methoxide ion.
9. PROTON_TRANSFER: hydrogen_atom=H_acid, from_atom=O_acid, to_atom=O_methoxide. A rapid proton transfer occurs from the newly formed formic acid to the methoxide ion, producing the final formate and methanol.

## Reimer-Tiemann Reaction

**Full Name:** Reimer-Tiemann Formylation
**Reaction equation:** C6H5OH + CHCl3 + 3NaOH -> C6H4(OH)CHO + 3NaCl + 2H2O

### Key Characteristics
- Converts phenol to ortho-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde).
- Reagents: Chloroform (CHCl3) and aqueous NaOH.
- Electrophile is Dichlorocarbene (:CCl2), generated in situ.

### Prototype: Phenol to Salicylaldehyde
Reaction name: Reimer-Tiemann Reaction of Phenol
Equation: Phenol + Chloroform + Base -> Salicylaldehyde
Reactants: Phenol (C6H5OH), Chloroform (CHCl3), Hydroxide (OH-)
Mechanism steps:
1. BASE_ABSTRACTION: base_atom=O_base, hydrogen_atom=H_chloroform. Hydroxide abstracts the acidic proton from chloroform.
2. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C_chloroform-Cl. A chloride ion leaves the CCl3- anion, generating the highly reactive neutral electrophile, dichlorocarbene (:CCl2).
3. BASE_ABSTRACTION: base_atom=O_base, hydrogen_atom=H_phenol. Another hydroxide abstracts the proton from phenol, forming a phenoxide ion.
4. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=C_ortho, electrophile_atom=C_carbene. The electron-rich ortho-carbon of the phenoxide ring attacks the empty p-orbital of the dichlorocarbene.
5. BOND_FORM: atom1=C_ortho, atom2=C_carbene, order=1. A carbon-carbon bond is formed, placing a negative charge on the carbene carbon.
6. PROTON_TRANSFER: hydrogen_atom=H_ortho, from_atom=C_ortho, to_atom=C_carbene. The proton from the ortho position shifts to the carbene carbon to restore the aromaticity of the benzene ring.
7. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=O_water, electrophile_atom=C_carbene. (Hydrolysis phase) Aqueous base hydrolyzes the two C-Cl bonds, eventually replacing them with a C=O (aldehyde) group to form salicylaldehyde.

## Williamson Ether Synthesis

**Full Name:** Williamson Ether Synthesis
**Reaction equation:** R-O- + R'-X -> R-O-R' + X-

### Key Characteristics
- Classic SN2 mechanism used to form both symmetrical and asymmetrical ethers.
- Reagents: An alkoxide ion (nucleophile) and a primary alkyl halide (electrophile).
- Fails or leads to elimination (E2) if a tertiary alkyl halide is used.

### Prototype: Sodium Ethoxide + Chloromethane
Reaction name: Williamson Synthesis of Ethyl Methyl Ether
Equation: CH3CH2O- + CH3Cl -> CH3CH2OCH3 + Cl-
Reactants: Ethoxide (CH3CH2O-), Chloromethane (CH3Cl)
Products: Ethyl methyl ether (CH3CH2OCH3), Chloride (Cl-)
Mechanism steps:
1. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=O_ethoxide, electrophile_atom=C_methyl. The negative oxygen of ethoxide attacks the primary carbon of chloromethane via backside attack.
2. BOND_FORM: atom1=O_ethoxide, atom2=C_methyl, order=1. A new ether C-O bond forms simultaneously as the leaving group exits.
3. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C_methyl-Cl. The carbon-chlorine bond breaks heterolytically.
4. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=Cl, new_charge=-1. Chlorine leaves as a stable chloride anion.

## Hydroboration-Oxidation

**Full Name:** Hydroboration-Oxidation of Alkenes
**Reaction equation:** R-CH=CH2 + BH3 -> (R-CH2-CH2)3B -> R-CH2-CH2-OH

### Key Characteristics
- Overall transformation is the Anti-Markovnikov hydration of an alkene.
- Syn-addition of water (H and OH add to the same face).
- No carbocation intermediate, thus no rearrangements occur (unlike acid-catalyzed hydration).

### Prototype: Propene to 1-Propanol
Mechanism involves a concerted 4-center transition state where Boron attaches to the less sterically hindered carbon, followed by oxidation with H2O2/NaOH which replaces Boron with an OH group with retention of stereochemistry.
