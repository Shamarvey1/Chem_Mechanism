## Cannizzaro Reaction

**Full Name:** Cannizzaro Disproportionation
**Also known as:** Cannizzaro reaction, aldehyde disproportionation
**Reaction equation:** 2 HCHO + NaOH -> HCOONa + CH3OH

### Key Characteristics
- Occurs in aldehydes lacking alpha-hydrogens (e.g., formaldehyde, benzaldehyde)
- Requires concentrated strong base (NaOH or KOH)
- Redox disproportionation: one aldehyde molecule is oxidized to carboxylic acid salt (formate), the other is reduced to alcohol (methanol)
- Rate-determining step is intermolecular hydride ion (H-) transfer

### Prototype: Formaldehyde Cannizzaro
Reaction name: Cannizzaro reaction of Formaldehyde
Equation: 2 HCHO + NaOH -> HCOONa + CH3OH
Reactants: Formaldehyde molecule 1 (HCHO), Formaldehyde molecule 2 (HCHO), Hydroxide (OH-)
Products: Sodium formate (HCOONa), Methanol (CH3OH)
Atoms in Formaldehyde 1: C1 (carbonyl carbon), O1 (carbonyl oxygen), H1 H2 (hydrogens on C1)
Bonds in Formaldehyde 1: bond-C1-O1 (order 2), bond-C1-H1 (order 1), bond-C1-H2 (order 1)
Atoms in Formaldehyde 2: C2 (carbonyl carbon), O2 (carbonyl oxygen), H3 H4 (hydrogens on C2)
Bonds in Formaldehyde 2: bond-C2-O2 (order 2), bond-C2-H3 (order 1), bond-C2-H4 (order 1)
Atoms in OH-: O3 (base oxygen, charge -1), H5 (base hydrogen)
Bonds in OH-: bond-O3-H5 (order 1)
Mechanism steps:
1. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=O3, electrophile_atom=C1. Hydroxide ion attacks carbonyl carbon C1 of the first formaldehyde.
2. BOND_FORM: atom1=O3, atom2=C1, order=1. Bond forms between hydroxide oxygen and C1.
3. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C1-O1. Carbonyl double bond opens to single bond.
4. BOND_FORM: atom1=C1, atom2=O1, order=1. Single bond forms tetrahedral intermediate.
5. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=O1, new_charge=-1. Alkoxide oxygen carries negative charge.
6. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C1-H2. C1-H2 bond breaks, releasing hydride ion (H-).
7. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C1-O1. C1-O1 single bond reforms carbonyl double bond.
8. BOND_FORM: atom1=C1, atom2=O1, order=2. Carbonyl double bond C=O reforms, creating formic acid.
9. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=H2, electrophile_atom=C2. Hydride ion attacks carbonyl carbon C2 of second formaldehyde.
10. BOND_FORM: atom1=H2, atom2=C2, order=1. Hydride attaches to C2.
11. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C2-O2. Carbonyl double bond of second formaldehyde opens.
12. BOND_FORM: atom1=C2, atom2=O2, order=1. Single bond forms methoxide ion.
13. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=O2, new_charge=-1. Methoxide oxygen carries negative charge.
14. PROTON_TRANSFER: hydrogen_atom=H5, from_atom=O3, to_atom=O2. Rapid proton transfer from formic acid to methoxide.
15. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-O3-H5. O-H bond of formic acid breaks.
16. BOND_FORM: atom1=O2, atom2=H5, order=1. Proton attaches to methoxide oxygen, completing methanol (CH3OH).
17. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=O2, new_charge=0. Methanol oxygen becomes neutral.
18. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=O3, new_charge=-1. Formate anion is stabilized.

## Williamson Ether Synthesis

**Full Name:** Williamson Ether Synthesis
**Also known as:** Williamson synthesis, ether preparation, alkoxide alkylation
**Reaction equation:** R-O- + R'-X -> R-O-R' + X-

### Key Characteristics
- Classic SN2 mechanism used to synthesize symmetrical and unsymmetrical ethers
- Alkoxide ion acts as nucleophile attacking primary alkyl halide
- Backside displacement of halide leaving group

### Prototype: Sodium Ethoxide + Chloromethane (Williamson)
Reaction name: Williamson Synthesis of Ethyl Methyl Ether
Equation: CH3CH2O- + CH3Cl -> CH3CH2OCH3 + Cl-
Reactants: Ethoxide ion (CH3CH2O-), Chloromethane (CH3Cl)
Products: Ethyl methyl ether (CH3CH2OCH3), Chloride ion (Cl-)
Atoms in Ethoxide: C1 (methyl carbon), C2 (CH2 carbon), O1 (alkoxide oxygen, charge -1), H1 H2 H3 (on C1), H4 H5 (on C2)
Bonds in Ethoxide: bond-C1-C2 (order 1), bond-C2-O1 (order 1), bond-C1-H1, bond-C1-H2, bond-C1-H3, bond-C2-H4, bond-C2-H5
Atoms in CH3Cl: C3 (methyl carbon), Cl1 (chlorine leaving group), H6 H7 H8 (on C3)
Bonds in CH3Cl: bond-C3-Cl1 (order 1), bond-C3-H6, bond-C3-H7, bond-C3-H8
Mechanism steps:
1. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C3-Cl1. The C-Cl bond breaks heterolytically.
2. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=Cl1, new_charge=-1. Chloride ion departs with -1 charge.
3. NUCLEOPHILE_ATTACK: nucleophile_atom=O1, electrophile_atom=C3. Alkoxide oxygen O1 attacks electrophilic carbon C3 via backside attack.
4. BOND_FORM: atom1=O1, atom2=C3, order=1. New ether carbon-oxygen bond forms, producing ethyl methyl ether.
5. CHARGE_CHANGE: atom=O1, new_charge=0. Ether oxygen becomes neutral.

## Hydroboration-Oxidation

**Full Name:** Hydroboration-Oxidation of Alkenes
**Also known as:** Hydroboration, anti-Markovnikov hydration of alkenes
**Reaction equation:** R-CH=CH2 + BH3 + H2O2/OH- -> R-CH2-CH2OH

### Key Characteristics
- Overall transformation is anti-Markovnikov hydration of an alkene
- Syn-addition: Boron and Hydrogen add simultaneously to the same face of the alkene
- Oxidation with alkaline H2O2 replaces Boron with OH group with retention of configuration
- No carbocation intermediate, preventing rearrangements

### Prototype: Ethylene Hydroboration-Oxidation
Reaction name: Hydroboration-oxidation of ethylene
Equation: CH2=CH2 + BH3 -> CH3CH2BH2
Reactants: Ethylene (CH2=CH2), Borane (BH3)
Products: Ethylborane (CH3CH2BH2)
Atoms in Ethylene: C1 C2, H1 H2 (on C1), H3 H4 (on C2)
Bonds in Ethylene: bond-C1-C2 (order 2), bond-C1-H1, bond-C1-H2, bond-C2-H3, bond-C2-H4
Atoms in BH3: B1 (boron), H5 H6 H7 (hydrogens on B1)
Bonds in BH3: bond-B1-H5 (order 1), bond-B1-H6 (order 1), bond-B1-H7 (order 1)
Mechanism steps:
1. ELECTROPHILE_ATTACK: electrophile_atom=B1, pi_atom1=C1, pi_atom2=C2. Boron atom attacks the alkene pi electrons in a concerted 4-center transition state.
2. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-C1-C2. Pi bond of ethylene opens.
3. BOND_FORM: atom1=C1, atom2=C2, order=1. Alkene becomes single bond.
4. BOND_BREAK: bond=bond-B1-H5. B-H bond breaks.
5. BOND_FORM: atom1=C1, atom2=B1, order=1. Boron attaches to C1.
6. BOND_FORM: atom1=C2, atom2=H5, order=1. Hydride transfers from boron to C2, completing syn-addition.
